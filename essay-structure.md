# Essay Structure

## Overall Argument Strategy

Use one connected identity theme across the assignment where possible. Part A chooses a scene where a marginalised identity shapes communication. Part B chooses a static advertisement that stereotypes a similar or connected identity. Part C analyses whether a professional body's social media reinforces or challenges similar patterns of belonging and authority.

The strongest assignment will not just describe examples. It will repeatedly answer this question:

> How does communication make some people appear more believable, competent, authoritative, safe, visible, or included than others?

## Suggested Working Thesis

Across interpersonal, visual, and professional communication, social identities influence who is treated as credible, competent, and belonging. Communication does this through speech patterns, visual representation, stereotypes, role allocation, visibility, and repeated social messages.

Adapt this thesis to your selected scene, advertisement, and profession.

### Paragraph 1: Context Analysis, About 100 Words

Purpose: introduce the scene and explain the identity-based context.

Suggested structure:

1. Name the scene, characters, and situation.
2. Identify the relevant social identities.
3. Explain the context that affects power, such as culture, institution, law, time period, family setting, workplace setting, or social expectations.
4. End with a sentence naming the main power dynamic you will analyse.

Sentence frame:

> In this scene, [character A] and [character B] communicate within [setting/context], where [identity factors] shape whose voice is treated as credible or legitimate. The interaction shows that power is produced not only through formal roles, but through [specific identity-based pattern].

### Paragraphs 2-4: Power Dynamic Analysis, About 400 Words

Purpose: analyse how communication reveals unequal power.

Recommended paragraph flow:

Paragraph 2: Who gets authority and credibility?

- Who speaks first or controls the topic?
- Who interrupts, questions, explains, or commands?
- Who is believed or doubted?
- Link the pattern to identity and one scholarly concept.

Paragraph 3: How verbal and non-verbal communication reinforces inequality.

- Analyse tone, silence, gaze, posture, facial expression, physical distance, emotional control, or body movement.
- Explain how the scene treats one person's knowledge, emotion, or experience as more valid than another's.
- Use a second scholarly source.

Paragraph 4: Broader social meaning.

- Connect the scene to wider inequality beyond the scene.
- Explain how the interaction reflects marginalisation, stereotyping, discrimination, privilege, or exclusion.
- Use a third scholarly source.
- Finish with a clear concluding sentence.

Part A mini-conclusion frame:

> Therefore, the scene demonstrates how [identity/system] shapes communication by making [privileged identity/group] appear [credible/normal/authoritative] while positioning [marginalised identity/group] as [less credible/silenced/dependent/excluded].

### Paragraph 1: Identify The Stereotype, About 80-100 Words

Purpose: identify the advertisement and state the stereotype.

Include:

- what the advertisement promotes;
- who is represented;
- which stereotype is communicated;
- how it connects to a marginalised identity.

Sentence frame:

> The advertisement promotes [product/service] by representing [group] as [stereotype]. This stereotype matters because it links [identity] with [assumption], which can shape how audiences understand competence, belonging, desirability, authority, safety, or dependence.

### Paragraphs 2-3: Visual Encoding, About 180-220 Words

Purpose: explain how the stereotype is communicated through visual choices.

Analyse specific features:

- composition and placement;
- centre/background positions;
- body posture and facial expression;
- clothing;
- colour and lighting;
- setting;
- objects and symbols;
- slogans, captions, or text;
- who appears active, passive, competent, dependent, threatening, emotional, or in control.

Useful structure:

1. Describe one visual feature.
2. Explain what meaning it creates.
3. Link the meaning to the stereotype.
4. Support with scholarly evidence where appropriate.

### Paragraph 4: Australian Social Impact, About 130-160 Words

Purpose: explain why repeated stereotypes matter in Australian society.

Include:

- one official or statistical source from Australia;
- at least one scholarly source;
- a clear link between repeated media messages and unequal outcomes.

Possible impact areas:

- workplace opportunity;
- pay;
- leadership;
- education;
- health;
- safety;
- public trust;
- social inclusion.

Sentence frame:

> Repeated messages like this can normalise the idea that [group] is [stereotyped trait], which affects [specific Australian issue]. This matters because [official source/data] shows [impact], while scholarly research explains that stereotypes can influence [attitudes/decisions/institutions].

### Final Short Paragraph: New Advertisement Rationale, About 50 Words

Purpose: explain your redesign.

Include:

- what you changed;
- how the new image still fits the original product/service/message;
- how it challenges the stereotype;
- why it avoids reintroducing another stereotype.

Sentence frame:

> My redesigned advertisement keeps the original focus on [product/service/message] but changes [representation choices]. By showing [group] as [alternative representation], it challenges the original stereotype that [old assumption] and instead presents [identity/group] as [competent/active/diverse/authoritative/included].

### Paragraph 1: Sample and Main Pattern, About 90-110 Words

Purpose: introduce the professional body, sample, and overall representation pattern.

Include:

- the organisation and platform;
- the date range;
- number of posts sampled;
- which professionals are repeatedly visible;
- which professionals appear absent or marginalised.

Sentence frame:

> I analysed [number] posts from [professional body] on [platform] between [date range]. Across the sample, [visible group/pattern] appeared most often in [leadership/expert/everyday professional] roles, while [underrepresented group] appeared [rarely/mainly in special posts/not clearly represented].

### Paragraph 2: Leadership, Expertise, and Visibility, About 130-150 Words

Purpose: analyse who is shown as competent, authoritative, and successful.

Consider:

- who is named;
- whose job titles are included;
- who receives awards or event promotion;
- who speaks as an expert;
- who is shown leading, teaching, presenting, or advising;
- who appears junior, passive, peripheral, or unnamed.

Use scholarly evidence to connect representation with perceived competence and belonging.

### Paragraph 3: Absence, Tokenism, or Limited Diversity, About 130-150 Words

Purpose: analyse who is missing and where diversity appears.

Consider:

- whether diversity appears across ordinary professional posts or only special events;
- whether some identities are not visible or not communicated;
- whether diversity is represented in leadership and expertise or only group photos;
- what repeated absence suggests about who belongs.

If identities such as LGBTQIA+ identity, Indigeneity, chronic illness, religion, or non-visible disability are not clearly visible or stated, you can say they are not clearly represented in the sample.

### Paragraph 4: Impact On Belonging, About 100-120 Words

Purpose: explain the implicit message and its consequences.

Include:

- what the feed suggests about who belongs and succeeds;
- how this may affect current professionals;
- how this may affect aspiring entrants;
- a connection to scholarly research.

Sentence frame:

> The implicit message is that [type of professional] is the profession's default image of success. This may support belonging for [group], but it can also make [underrepresented group] feel peripheral or less likely to imagine themselves as future leaders in the field.

### Final Paragraph: Allyship Recommendation, 60-80 Words

Purpose: provide a practical action for the professional body.

Strong recommendation qualities:

- directly responds to the pattern found;
- is specific and achievable;
- frames inclusion as an ongoing communication practice;
- avoids vague phrases such as "be more diverse".

Sentence frame:

> As an allyship action, [professional body] should [specific action], ensuring that [underrepresented group] are shown not only in diversity-focused posts but also in everyday expert, leadership, and achievement content. This would help communicate that professional competence and belonging are not limited to [dominant visible group].

Useful concept areas:

- representation;
- media effects;
- professional identity;
- diversity and inclusion.

## Final Assembly Structure

Use this order in the final submission:

1. Student details.
2. Part A screenshot, URL, timestamp, analysis, references, and word count.
3. Part B original advertisement, URL, access date, analysis, redesigned advertisement, rationale, references, and word count.
4. Part C feed screenshots, platform and handle, URL, date range, sample size, analysis, allyship recommendation, references, and word count.

## Quality Control Checklist

- Every paragraph answers the assignment question, not just describes content.
- Each part names a specific identity-based power issue.
- Claims about inequality are supported by scholarly or official evidence.
- Part B includes Australian evidence.
- Part C identifies repeated patterns across a sample, not isolated examples.
- APA in-text citations match the reference list.
- All references are real and checked.
- Guidance text from the original template is deleted before submission.
- Final file is exported as PDF and correctly named.
