# Assessment 1: Cultural Intelligence Reflection

## Overview

This individual reflective assessment requires students to critically evaluate their intercultural development and Cultural Intelligence (CQ) using their CQ T1 assessment results. The reflection should be written in the first person, for example:

- I observed...
- I realised...
- I plan to...

The report should examine intercultural experiences and evolving perceptions, analyse the student's CQ profile by identifying strengths and development areas, and consider how CQ influences the ability to work effectively in culturally diverse environments.

The report must also include an evidence-based CQ development plan with SMART goals targeting specific CQ dimensions. Students should analyse their CQ assessment data and evaluate how digital tools or emerging technologies, such as generative AI, can support ongoing CQ development and professional practice.

Through this assessment, students demonstrate reflective practice, professional communication, data literacy, and the application of cultural intelligence in global business contexts.

## Unit Learning Outcomes

On successful completion of this assessment, students will be able to:

1. Identify factors, including knowledge, attitudes, and skills, that can influence the effectiveness of communication in a range of cultural settings and with people from diverse cultural backgrounds.
2. Explain how these factors contribute to the complexities of communicating with individuals, groups, and organisations from other cultures.
3. Evaluate the capabilities and limitations that they and others bring to interactions in varying cultural contexts.

## Required Headings

The reflection should use the following report-style headings.

## 1. Intercultural Self-Awareness and Experience

Reflect on previous experiences engaging with people from cultures different from your own.

Consider:

- how your perceptions have developed over time;
- how your behaviours or responses have changed in culturally diverse situations;
- what you have learned about your own assumptions, communication style, and intercultural awareness.

## 2. Critical Analysis of Your CQ T1 Results

Analyse your CQ T1 profile and consider what it reveals about your current intercultural capabilities.

Include:

- your strengths across the CQ dimensions;
- your areas for development across the CQ dimensions;
- what the results reveal about your intercultural capabilities;
- examples from your own experiences to support your analysis;
- how your CQ profile may influence your effectiveness when working in culturally diverse teams and professional environments.

## 3. Digital and Data-Informed Professional Development

Interpret your CQ T1 results and consider how digital tools such as generative AI, including tools such as Copilot, could support your ongoing CQ development.

Instructions:

1. Upload or summarise your CQ T1 results into an AI tool.
2. Do not provide the AI tool with any personal identifying information.
3. Prompt the AI tool to generate recommendations for improving three or more CQ dimensions.
4. Critically evaluate the AI-generated recommendations.

Your evaluation should consider both benefits and limitations of digital tools and emerging technologies.

Possible points to discuss:

- how AI can help interpret patterns in CQ data;
- how AI can suggest development strategies;
- whether AI recommendations are specific, realistic, or culturally sensitive;
- risks such as generic advice, bias, privacy concerns, or over-reliance on technology.

## 4. Evidence-Based CQ Development Plan

Develop a practical CQ development plan informed by your CQ T1 results.

As a minimum, include:

- one short-term goal to be achieved within the semester;
- one long-term goal to be achieved within the next 12-14 months.

Goals may target multiple CQ dimensions:

- CQ Drive;
- CQ Knowledge;
- CQ Strategy;
- CQ Action.

Each goal should meet the SMART criteria:

- Specific;
- Measurable;
- Achievable;
- Relevant;
- Time-bound.

## 5. Applying CQ to Professional Practice

Evaluate the relevance of cultural intelligence to your future studies, career, and professional relationships.

Discuss:

- how CQ may support your future academic work;
- how CQ may support your career development;
- how CQ may improve professional relationships;
- what personal value you gained from completing the CQ T1 assessment and reflecting on the results.

## Professional Communication Requirements

- The report should be written in report style.
- Key headings should be used to improve readability.
- Academic references are required when referring to key theories, concepts, or frameworks.
- References should follow an accepted academic referencing convention, such as APA or Harvard.
- The report must be edited carefully for grammar, spelling, and punctuation.
- The report must include a cover page listing:
  - name;
  - student number;
  - tutor.

## Word Count Notes

- The report should be no more than 1500 words.
- Tables, appendix, and references are not included in the word count.
- An appendix is optional and should only be used for information essential to the body of the report.

## Submission Notes

- Submit electronically via Canvas.
- Submit in Word format.
- This assessment qualifies for the 48-hour late submission period.

## Checklist

- Reflection is written in first person.
- Report is approximately 1500 words.
- Cover page includes name, student number, and tutor.
- All five required headings are included.
- CQ T1 results are analysed, not just described.
- Strengths and development areas across CQ dimensions are identified.
- Personal intercultural experiences are used as examples.
- Generative AI or another digital tool is used to generate CQ development recommendations.
- AI-generated recommendations are critically evaluated.
- At least one short-term SMART goal is included.
- At least one long-term SMART goal is included.
- CQ is connected to future studies, career, and professional relationships.
- Academic concepts, theories, or frameworks are referenced.
- Referencing follows APA or Harvard style.
- Report is proofread before submission.
